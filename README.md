That gap is the part most buyers find out about too late.

## What "telescopic" means on the label

The name is L'Oréal's shorthand for stretch. The original product was built around a patented flexible Precision Brush with two working sides: a flat side that drags formula from root to tip to extend the look of the lash, and a comb side that separates lashes so the length doesn't turn into a clump.

L'Oréal's own product page for Telescopic Original states up to 70% longer-looking lashes and cites an instrumental test of 27 subjects after 30 passes of the mascara. Some regional product pages and retailer listings quote 60% instead. Same family, different market copy, and the number depends on which test the local team is citing. Treat the figure as a marketing claim on a lengthening mascara, not a measurement you can verify at home.

The more useful fact: this entire line is a **lengthening** range. If you want thick, dense volume, you are looking at the wrong shelf.

## Matching the version to your lashes

**Straight, fine or downward-pointing lashes, and you want length without weight:** the original-style tube, whether it's sold as Original, Clean Definition or plain Telescopic Mascara in your market. Thin comb brush, root-to-tip stretch, natural-looking separation.

**You want lift and hold, and you're willing to fight the brush:** Telescopic Lift. The double-hook wand grabs and loads lashes, and the ceramide story is about holding the lift rather than adding bulk. Be aware that this is the most divisive tube in the line. Adore Beauty shows it at 3.6 out of 5 with roughly 53% to 57% of reviewers recommending it, and Lookfantastic sits at 4.08 out of 5 across 114 reviews with a recurring complaint about the brush being awkward or sharp. One of the most common criticisms is transfer to the under-eye by the end of the day.

**You want the biggest visible lash-lift effect and you care more about curl than volume:** Telescopic Extensionist. Good Housekeeping UK's tester rated it above a salon lash lift for length and curl, describing a lifted, eye-opening result and no flaking through the day. It launched exclusively on TikTok Shop in the US in September 2025 and reached European beauty counters in 2026, so availability still varies by country. The UK Amazon price of £6.66 against an £11.66 RRP is the cheapest verified number in this whole line.

**You want the colour-pop versions:** the tinted Lifts. Reviews are mixed and worth reading before you commit. Amazon reviewers on the Galactic Purple shade report patchy colour payoff, clumping and, in several cases, itching. Nobody in those reviews is arguing the tint is subtle.

## What the reviews consistently flag

Mascara reviews are noisy, but a few patterns hold across retailers.

**The original wand is fragile.** Amazon's aggregated review tags for the original 8 ml tube list 72 mentions of wand durability, and only 8 of them are positive. Bending, cracking and difficulty getting the wand back into the tube come up repeatedly. Whether that's the trade-off for a thin comb design or just weak plastic is a fair argument.

**Clumping is real and technique-dependent.** The same aggregated tags show 173 mentions of clumping, split 113 positive to 60 negative. The formula is not forgiving of a rushed application.

**Removal splits people.** 105 mentions of removability, 65 positive and 40 negative. Some reviewers describe it coming off cleanly with a standard remover; others say micellar water takes several passes. If you wear contact lenses or have sensitive eyes, buy the version your market sells as the washable original rather than assuming every Telescopic tube behaves the same way.

**Tubes go thick before they go empty.** Reviewers and at least one independent round-up describe the texture thickening noticeably after roughly six to eight weeks. One Canadian reviewer reported a visible drop in performance in under 20 uses. That's a worse-than-average case, not the norm, but it's a real complaint rather than an outlier.

And the structural one: the line does not do volume. Lookfantastic reviewers say it outright, and Adore Beauty's most-cited criticism of Clean Definition is that it lengthens but doesn't hold a curl. If your lash problem is thin density, this family won't solve it.

### Application notes that come from the brand, not from me

The official routine is short and it matters more here than with a fat-brush mascara:

1. Start with clean, residue-free lashes.
2. Place the brush at the base and sweep upward using the flat side for length.
3. Flip to the comb side and separate lash by lash.
4. Wait a beat between coats rather than layering wet on wet.
5. Don't pump the wand in and out of the tube; it pushes air in and dries the formula out faster.

## The price spread is bigger than the product differences

Here is the part that decides what you actually pay.

The same 8 ml Clean Definition tube is listed at AU$14.99 at Chemist Warehouse, against an RRP of AU$29.99. Adore Beauty lists it at AU$29.99. Beautycrew puts the RRP for the Australian Telescopic Mascara at AU$26.95. Three numbers, one product, and a gap of roughly double between the cheapest and the dearest.

The UK shows the same pattern: £7.26 for the original against an RRP of £9.16, and £6.66 for Extensionist against an £11.66 RRP.

None of that is a secret discount. It's the normal drugstore cycle. Mass retailers discount the tube hard during promo windows, beauty specialists list it nearer RRP, and the manufacturer's suggested price sits above both. If you buy the first listing you find, the odds of paying near RRP are good.

So the buying decision has two parts, and they cost different amounts of effort:

- **Which tube** is a five-minute read of the table above, plus an honest look at your own lashes.
- **What you pay for it** depends on checking two or three retailers instead of one.

## Questions that come up a lot

**Is telescopic mascara waterproof?**
Not by default. The standard tubes are washable formulas, and both the original and Lift have separate waterproof variants sold under their own listings. Check the variant name before you pay.

**Does it hold a curl?**
On the original-style tube, reviews are split, and the most cited criticism is that it lengthens without holding a curl. The curl and lift claims sit with Lift and Extensionist, which are the tubes built around hold rather than pure stretch.

**How long does a tube last?**
Reviews point to texture thickening somewhere around six to eight weeks of regular use. Mascara generally has a shorter practical life than its three-month shelf-life guidance suggests, so buy the size you'll actually finish.

**Is it a real dupe for Prada Pradascope?**
A widely shared creator comparison put Telescopic's long, separated, lifted finish close to the Pradascope look at a fraction of the price, while conceding the luxury option wins on packaging and feel. That's one person's side-by-side, not a lab result, but it lines up with the general reputation of the line as a length-focused drugstore workhorse.

**Will it survive crying or a humid commute?**
Mixed. Some reviewers describe it as nearly smudge-proof once dry, others report transfer to the under-eye by the end of the day, particularly if they coat the lower lashes. Use less on the bottom lash line if you're prone to it.
